# Director-Control Keyword Library

Use these phrases as building blocks, not as an unmotivated keyword pile. Select only what the shot can visibly support.

## Identity and asset locks

- `canonical face identity` — one approved facial source is authoritative.
- `stable facial geometry through head rotation` — facial proportions remain stable during turns.
- `unchanged silhouette and body proportions` — preserve recognizability in wide shots.
- `wardrobe construction remains identical` — seams, closures, layers, length, footwear, and accessories do not drift.
- `prop remains in the same hand and state` — preserve hand occupancy and open/closed/damaged state.
- `rear design follows the canonical back-view sheet` — use for turns and back-facing action.
- `distinct NPC identity matrix` — vary age, face shape, hair, body, wardrobe, occupation, color, and behavior.

## Spatial and continuity locks

- `fixed location map` — foreground, midground, background, entrances, exits, and landmarks stay fixed.
- `stable screen direction` — preserve left-to-right or right-to-left travel.
- `eyeline match` — gaze direction connects correctly across the cut.
- `action match` — the outgoing action phase continues from the incoming pose.
- `light-direction match` — key, fill, rim, and practical-source direction remain consistent.
- `sound bridge across the cut` — an existing sound carries the transition.
- `no spontaneous entrance or disappearance` — every subject has a visible or motivated entry/exit.

## FOV and lens-feel ladder

Use field of view when a precise optical control is helpful:

| Horizontal FOV | Approximate feel | Typical use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 107° | extreme ultra-wide | distorted scale, overwhelming environment |
| 84° | ultra-wide | clear geography and deep staging |
| 63° | wide-natural | active two-shot, movement with context |
| 47° | neutral | balanced human perspective |
| 29° | short telephoto | portrait separation, compressed relation |
| 18° | telephoto | surveillance, isolation, strong compression |
| 12° | long telephoto | distant detail, flattened depth |
| 8° | extreme telephoto | highly selective distant information |

Do not use a wide FOV for a face-critical close-up unless distortion is intentional. Use 63°–47° for geography and readable group blocking; 29°–18° for identity-critical reactions and facial stability.

## Camera movement

- `locked-off static frame` — still camera; tension comes from blocking.
- `slow push-in to the decision point` — compress attention as thought becomes action.
- `measured pull-out to reveal consequence` — expand the frame after a decision.
- `single lateral truck` — reveal a relationship while preserving geography.
- `motivated pan following the gaze` — transfer attention to what the character sees.
- `subject-locked tracking` — follow movement while preserving scale and screen direction.
- `controlled arc around the power shift` — reveal a relational reversal.
- `low pedestal rise` — disclose scale or status without changing lens.
- `restrained handheld micro-drift` — slight human instability, not random shake.
- `camera settles before the final beat` — arrive at a stable ending composition.

Always specify movement direction, amplitude, speed, subject relationship, and stopping point when they matter.

## Focus and attention

- `foreground occlusion motivates the reveal`
- `single rack focus from foreground evidence to midground action`
- `second rack focus lands on the emotional subject`
- `focus holds through the gesture`
- `deep focus preserves the spatial relationship`
- `shallow focus isolates the reaction without losing the eyeline`

Do not use repeated focus shifts as decoration. Each shift must transfer narrative attention.

## Performance direction

Replace abstract emotions with playable behavior:

- `eyes acquire the target before the head turns`
- `jaw tightens; lips stay closed`
- `breath catches, then releases through the nose`
- `shoulders rise defensively before settling`
- `fingers curl around the object, then loosen one by one`
- `weight shifts from the rear foot to the lead foot`
- `the body protects the impact site before conscious control suppresses the expression`
- `a half-beat hesitation precedes commitment`
- `the smile reaches the eyes only after the threat passes`
- `gaze breaks first; the torso follows later`

For pain, show impact site, protective reflex, breath interruption, muscle contraction, delay, compensation, and recovery. A neutral face does not erase involuntary body response.

## Action and physics

- `clear anticipation before force`
- `one dominant line of action`
- `readable contact point`
- `momentum continues after impact`
- `weight transfers through the planted foot`
- `center of gravity leaves the support base`
- `cloth and hair follow with delayed overlap`
- `recovery begins only after balance is re-established`
- `material deformation matches the contact force`
- `dust, debris, or liquid reacts after the primary impact`

Use the chain: `intention → anticipation → exertion → contact/near-miss → reaction → recovery → consequence`.

## Lighting, color, and material

- `fixed white balance at 5600K` or another explicit Kelvin value.
- `key light remains frame-left at 45 degrees`
- `practical light is visible and motivates the key`
- `controlled highlight roll-off on skin and metal`
- `stable exposure through camera movement`
- `palette remains within named dominant and accent colors`
- `material response remains distinct: matte cloth, brushed metal, glossy glass, translucent energy`
- `destination-world rendering rules replace only the environment treatment while character anchors remain recognizable`

## Timing and edit

- `action begins before the cut and completes after it`
- `hold two beats after the revelation`
- `cut on the thought, not merely on movement`
- `sound arrives one beat before the visual reveal`
- `the final 0.5 seconds settle into the supplied last frame`
- `one uninterrupted developing shot`
- `no decorative cutaway`

## Sound direction

- `stable room tone establishes scale`
- `sync foley defines weight and material`
- `off-screen sound expands unseen space`
- `subjective high-frequency roll-off after impact`
- `dialogue occupies a clear mid-frequency window`
- `music ducks under essential dialogue`
- `brief silence isolates the irreversible beat`
- `sound bridge carries the cut`
- `audience-only score uses named instruments, tempo, rhythm, and dynamics`

## Positive lock sentence pattern

Write stable-state constraints as present facts:

```text
The same character identity, hair shape, eye colors, wardrobe construction, footwear, and carried prop remain unchanged throughout the shot. The doorway stays frame-left, the key light remains from frame-right, and every subject remains present until a motivated exit.
```

Add only short H3-specific exclusions after the positive description:

```text
No identity drift, duplicated people, extra limbs, prop morphing, mirrored layout, random text, or disappearing companion.
```
